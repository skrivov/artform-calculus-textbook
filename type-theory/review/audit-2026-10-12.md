# Independent review of Five Roles for Artforms

Reviewer: Codex/Astra, 12 October 2026. Reviewed the paper and `ArtformRoles`
at `2357f6f`, including the updates through `743171f`, against edition 54.

**Verdict: return the paper for revision. Accept the Lean library's stated
policy, graph and counting results within their explicit scope.** A passing
kernel check does not establish the stronger role-minimality claim or the
faithfulness of the newly added state/capture encodings. Findings 1-3 below
must be addressed before the paper receives an unqualified approval.

This is separate from the approved
[edition-54 migration](../../textbook/review/edition-54-2026-10-12.md).
No existing textbook approval depends on the paper's claims, and the library
has no `@[book]` entries.

## Findings requiring revision

### 1. [P1] The counts do not prove that two role indices are necessary

Location: `paper/role-stratified-artform-type-theory.tex:739-750`, Theorem
4.4(ii) and its proof; the stronger claim is repeated in the introduction,
conclusion and validation tables (notably lines 853 and 3285).

The checked result `twoCorners_counts` proves the three numerical answers
3, 2 and 1. It does not prove the crucial sentence that a single role for
applications and certificates “would have to” count both by one of the two
wrong conventions. Role equality does not force type equality or the same
counting/erasure operation. One role can contain an application type counted
by inhabitants and a certificate family used only through inhabitation.
The paper already permits many distinct families at one role.

Concrete countermodel: identify SCRIPT with WITNESS in the role labels,
retain the distinct `App` and `Ev` families and the original definition of
`Law.histories`. Every policy entry is preserved, and the two-corner answer
is still 2. `Histories.lean` does not import the role policy and its `Law`
does not contain a role field. I also checked in Lean:

```lean
def collapseRole : Role -> Role
  | .script => .witness
  | r => r

example (a b : Role) :
    demand (collapseRole a) (collapseRole b) = demand a b := by
  cases a <;> cases b <;> rfl

example : twoCorners.histories 0 1 1 = 2 := by decide
```

The numerical part of 4.4(ii) is correct; its interpretation as a necessity
theorem is unsupported. Either describe the counts as motivation for keeping
two semantic jobs, or define a restricted notion of role merger which really
forces uniform counting and prove the claim under that explicit restriction.
Do not advertise the 3/2/1 computation as a Lean proof that exactly five roles
are mathematically necessary. Principle 2.2 fixes the vocabulary; it does not
by itself prove a minimality or exhaustiveness theorem about all calculi.

### 2. [P1] The state encoding retains legality-proof multiplicity

Location: `paper/role-stratified-artform-type-theory.tex:3028-3038`, contrasted
with lines 327-330 and Appendix B, lines 3654-3656.

Section 2.2 correctly says that the book does not count different legality
proofs as different states. Section 12.5 instead declares an unrestricted
witness family `Legal_A(t)` and defines `State(A)` as an ordinary dependent
sum over it. The calculus explicitly has no proof-irrelevance or quotient
equation.

Its own signature rules admit a closed table `t` and two distinct primitive
constants `l0, l1 : Legal_A(t)`: both have closed, grade-free, family-headed
types. Consequently `(t,l0)` and `(t,l1)` are distinct normal forms of
`State(A)`. No reduction/equality rule identifies them. Thus the displayed
encoding alone does not implement the book's legal-assignment state count.

Keep the well-formed WITNESS sum, but explicitly supply a semantics/equivalence
for observing and counting states through their table, with the required
invariance, or a justified restriction that makes legality proof-irrelevant.
If that belongs outside the present calculus, state the boundary and stop
claiming this encoding already realizes the book's property-valued legality.
Do not silently add a proof-irrelevance axiom to the existing metatheory.

### 3. [P1] The capture action bypasses its applicability certificate

Location: `paper/role-stratified-artform-type-theory.tex:3118-3143`; action
declarations at lines 3048-3074 and applicability at lines 3087-3092.

The book says a guarded move is total on its admitted input, including the
needed evidence (`chapters/01-foundations.tex:5087-5089`). Here `nxe5` has
type `Script(A,A)`, and `act nxe5 s` is well typed for every `s : State(A)`
without a term of `Applicable(nxe5,s)`. The declared `act_L` consumes proof
that the *input state* is legal, not proof that this capture is applicable.
Declaring an unused applicability family cannot enforce that guard.

For example, keep the declared action primitives and give
`Applicable(nxe5,s)` no inhabitant. The claimed next state remains well
typed. This is also why the final claim that effects match Table 4 “item for
item” fails: the next-state expression has no dependency on the capture
certificate or its attack-map evidence, whereas `ChessItem.edge` explicitly
includes `certificate -> next`.

Make the capture's source the admitted-state type or define a guarded action
which consumes the corresponding certificate, then show the effect mapping.
Distinguish a schematic role-valid graph from an implementation of an actual
legal chess capture. The current graph validity theorem remains correct.

## Accepted results and readings

- **Book fidelity:** Table 1's roles and the state-as-WITNESS reading match
  edition 54. This is appropriate for states of a fixed frame; a growing
  carrier has forms for its stages. D4 is now an explicit book convention,
  supported by the connection passage and process examples. It does not make
  all evidence proof-irrelevant, and it does not resolve finding 2 by itself.
- **Requirements and policy:** the free/gated/absent matrix agrees with the
  book's revised downstream prose. Theorem 2.5 and `demandOf_eq_demand`
  establish uniqueness **among the displayed level-generated policies**
  satisfying R1-R4. They are not a uniqueness theorem for arbitrary possible
  dependency disciplines. The finite role table, grade operations, path
  closure and nine separated pairs faithfully match their Lean forms.
- **Local to global:** Theorem 3.2 has the diagonal-zero hypothesis required
  by the empty chain; Lean includes it. The converse's three-item construction
  establishes path closure, with no hidden stronger graph assumption.
- **Value guarantees:** determination and Theorems 3.8-3.10 match Lean,
  including acyclicity, local computation, consistent valuations, agreement
  of the appropriate computing functions, and agreement at the **sources**
  of the gates. The hypotheses are substantive, not automatic invariance of
  arbitrary changed computations.
- **Records in the graph:** `crosses_gate` proves a gated edge on a live
  route and reachability of its target. Treating that edge/target as recorded
  use is the graph abstraction. It is not a mechanization of event-bearing
  gate contraction or payload retention in the dependent calculus.
- **Counts:** Definition 4.1 matches `Law.histories`, `proofs`, and
  `trajectories`, including zero-certificate applications. The two uniqueness
  hypotheses for equality of counts match Proposition 4.2. The 3/2/1 example
  and square walker are correct; `walker_counts_agree` now quantifies over
  every starting corner, time and endpoint.
- **Examples:** chess and realizer graph declarations match their printed
  tables and their valid/invalid variants. They are explicit examples of
  role-valid dependencies, not a verification of full chess rules or a
  general operational realizer.

## Part II and the new signature declarations

After normalizing the declared renaming (`Obj -> Frm`, `Str -> Prs`, policy
`P -> D`, receipt -> record), the core syntax/signature/type/term rules are
unchanged apart from a prose capitalization. The reduction and metatheory
sections differ only in the expected terminology and explanatory prose. The
complete rule-set and reduction/equality appendices are identical under that
renaming. This accepts preservation of the earlier calculus; it is not a
fresh mechanization of its paper proofs.

The new `Table`/`Legal` families, homogeneous WITNESS sum, `Script`, `id`,
`comp`, and the split `act_T`/`act_L` are admitted by the displayed signature
rules. In particular their primitive results are family-headed; `act` is a
derived pair, not an illegal primitive with a sum-headed result. `Applicable`
is a well-formed witness family. The `note` gate's ordinary score parameter,
witness payload and protected payload-independent result meet Sig-Gate;
`attack` and `eval` have the stated family-headed results and ordinary edges.
Well-formedness does not establish the semantic claims returned in 2 and 3.

The interpretation example's move from presentation endpoints to FORM is
admitted: form parameters are ordinary into witness families and into the
installed presentation; exact attestation still targets the homogeneous
witness pair; the gate's result is grade-free and independent of its payload.
Its admission table remains valid.

COHERENCE decisions T1-T4, T6 and T7 now describe implemented book changes;
T5 was correctly withdrawn for growing carriers. Its item T1 still says
“It now says” before quoting the superseded text: make the historical tense
explicit during revision. The claims in D3 about role merger need finding 1's
qualification, and D4's state encoding needs finding 2's boundary. The new
book citations also need the corrections in the edition-migration review.
