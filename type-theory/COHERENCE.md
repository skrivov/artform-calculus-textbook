# The paper and the textbook: one theory of the five roles

Written 2026-10-11, when the paper `paper/role-stratified-artform-type-theory.tex`
was rebuilt from the manuscript *Traceable Gates for Artforms* (September
2026) to agree with the textbook *Artform Calculus*. Book references are to
the edition of 11 October 2026 (53rd), by section and label. Revised
2026-10-12 after the paper's review of that day: D3 and D4 carry its
qualifications, and items 8 and 9 of §3 come from the edition-54 review.
Revised again the same day: §4 compares the definitions of the two Lean
libraries, once every definition of the book had a Lean form.

## 1. Where the two disagreed

1. **Names and readings.** The manuscript wrote the roles `O, S, W, T, R` and
   read them as constructed objects, installed structure, evidence,
   transformations and renderings. The book's names are FORM, PRESENTATION,
   WITNESS, SCRIPT, RENDERING, with the readings of the table in §1.9
   (`sec:five-roles`).
2. **Four classes, not five.** In the policy matrix the WITNESS and SCRIPT
   rows are equal, and so are their columns. The manuscript gave scripts no
   rule of their own: a script was an opaque constant, and its action on
   states was outside the calculus. So the manuscript justified four roles.
3. **States.** The manuscript typed `State(A)` at the structure
   (presentation) role. The book gives three answers. §1.9 says *name the
   presentation and then say what inhabits it*. `ROLE-LEDGER.md` §4 says the
   connection *is a state, not carried structure*. §5.9
   (`sec:connection-witness`) says the gathered witness family *acquired no
   new role*. And the process laboratory says *evidence and choice are one
   role*.
4. **"Receipt".** The manuscript called the node a gate contraction leaves a
   *receipt*. The book uses *receipt* for a witness of a measured feature
   (Proposition 3.43, `prop:metric-feature-receipts-book`).
5. **One entry of the policy is missing from the book's prose.** The matrix
   gates presentation → form (`D(P,F) = 1`). The prose in `sec:role-policy`
   names only witnesses and scripts as feeding back.
6. **Readouts.** The book says that computing a readout is a SCRIPT and its
   displayed result a RENDERING (`sec:rods-grids`). The manuscript typed a
   readout function by its codomain, a rendering.
7. **Rules and roles.** The book keeps them as two disciplines (§1.11,
   `sec:rules-view`). The manuscript combines them in one judgement but did
   not say so in the book's terms.

## 2. The decisions now in the paper

- **D1. Names and readings** are the book's. The symbols are
  `F, P, W, S, R`, and the policy matrix is called `D`, because `P` now
  names a role.
- **D2. The policy is the book's matrix, unchanged.** It is generated by four
  levels: carrier (F), carried structure (P), instance (W and S), and display
  (R). Four requirements stated in the book force it: readings are terminal;
  the frame does not change while a run proceeds; no addressing is part of
  the board; and a certificate may mention a move while a guarded move takes
  its certificate. The policy is path closed, and path closure is exactly
  the condition under which checking each direct dependency suffices.
  (Lean: `demandOf_eq_demand`, `demand_pathClosed`, `Construction.Valid.reach`,
  `Construction.pathClosed_of_reach`.)
- **D3. WITNESS and SCRIPT share a level and must.** The book needs both
  directions free. A certificate names its move; reversibility is a property
  of a shear; a guarded move takes its certificate; `SpatialRead(w)` is a
  script built from a witness. Their rules separate them. A history is a
  list of applications of scripts, while a certificate only admits an
  application and is counted as a property (§7.4, `qr:composition`).
  When each role's data are counted one way, merging the roles changes the
  book's counts: on the two-corner law the three counts are 3 (proofs
  counted), 2 (the book's count) and 1 (endpoints only). Without that
  restriction the counts motivate two roles for two jobs; they do not prove
  that every calculus needs both, since identifying SCRIPT with WITNESS keeps
  every entry of the policy (review of 2026-10-12, finding 1; paper Theorem
  4.5(ii) and Remark 4.6). (Lean: `policySeparates_iff`,
  `witness_script_same_policy`, `twoCorners_counts`, `merged_roles_miscount`,
  `book_count_separates`, `collapseRole_keeps_policy`.)
- **D4. A state is witness data.** A state of a frame is a legal choice of
  values at its positions: a table with the evidence that it is legal,
  which is the book's own formation rule (`def:frame-instance`). Choices
  and evidence are witness data, so the state space is a homogeneous
  witness-role sum in the calculus. STATE is still not a sixth role. This
  agrees with the ledger, with §5.9 and with the process laboratory. It
  overrules §1.9's *name the presentation*. A process that grows its
  carrier, such as Chapter 3's growth, has forms for its stages, and each
  step of growth is a gate into a form. The calculus keeps legality
  proof-relevant: the sum counts a table once per legality proof, two proofs
  of one table give two distinct states, and the book's count of legal tables
  is a count through the table; the calculus adds no proof-irrelevance rule
  (review finding 2; paper §12.5). (Lean: `card_states_eq_iff`,
  `tables_of_states`, `Book.Chess.two_legal_states`.)
- **D5. Gates record what was assumed.** A gate contraction leaves a
  *record*, not a receipt. Gates are the only crossings from the instance
  level into the frame, and from presentation into form.
- **D6. The codomain decides.** A value has the role of the type at which it
  is judged. A declared readout script is SCRIPT and its values are
  RENDERING. The squares one side bears on are a derived state, which is
  witness data; an engine's evaluation is a rendering.
- **D7. A role is a stage.** A gate retypes a datum and its node retains the
  datum. A supplied policy is witness data; installed in a realizer, it is
  presentation.
- **D8. The four useful words** are formal. A state is witness data. A
  policy is witness data, and presentation once a gate installs it. A reduct
  is a script's result. An observable is a script whose values are
  renderings.
- **D9. Rules and roles in one judgement.** The calculus's judgement carries
  both: the rules are Martin-Löf's, and the index is the role.

## 3. The textbook passages these decisions affect

The paper was changed first. The book was mostly consistent with the
decisions already; these were the places where it was not. The author
accepted T1 and T2 and asked for all the recommendations (2026-10-11). The
fifty-fourth edition of the book (its packet
`planning/lean-repository-2026-10-11/`) applies T1–T4, T6 and T7 and cites
this repository; T5 is withdrawn (below).

1. **§1.9, *Four useful words that are not roles*, the state paragraph**
   (`chapters/01-foundations.tex`, after `\subsection{Four useful words that
   are not roles}`). The fifty-third edition said: *When a role must be named,
   name the presentation and then say what inhabits it.* Under D4 this was
   wrong, and the fifty-fourth edition replaced it. The proposed
   replacement was: *A state is a legal choice of values at the
   positions of a frame: a table together with the evidence that it is
   legal. Choices and evidence are witness data, so when a role must be named
   for a state, it is WITNESS; the state set is not another role.* This was
   the one change the coherent theory needed.
2. **§1.9, `sec:role-policy`, first paragraph.** Add the presentation → form
   gate, for example: *A witness or a script may even feed back into a
   presentation or a form, and a presentation into a form, provided the
   passage goes through a gate that records what was assumed.*
3. **§1.9, the five-role table, WITNESS row** (optional). *Evidence that a
   comparison or move is allowed* could add *and the choices it certifies*,
   with *a state* among the examples.
4. **§1.9 checkpoint, answers (c) and (g)** (optional). (c) *derived but not
   displayed: witness data, a state of a derived frame, available to a
   rule*. (g) *part of the state, which is witness data; a state is not a
   role*.
5. **`chapters/03-geometry-laboratory.tex`, rolestrip at the batch step**
   (*FORM rule and states*). *Withdrawn on reading the context:* the states
   there are the stages of a growth, which are forms, so the rolestrip is
   right as it stands (D4's last sentence).
6. **`ROLE-LEDGER.md`** (a planning file, not printed). §1 should take the
   paper's readings, and §3 (STATE) should take D4. The matrix in §2 is
   unchanged.
7. **§1.11, *What the rules do not see*** (later). Once the book cites the
   repository, this is where it can point to the paper's single judgement.

8. **§1.9, the crossing sentence** (`sec:role-policy`; edition-54 review,
   correction 1). It says
   that every crossing into a form or presentation passes a gate. A form may
   be used freely by a presentation (`D(F,P) = 0`; the chess construction
   has the free edge board → addressing). Proposed: *every crossing from the
   instance level into the frame, and from a presentation into a form,
   passes a gate.* Record retention is now checked in Lean for the calculus
   (`record_persistence`, `gate_adequacy`), so the book may attribute it to
   the paper's mechanized calculus rather than to a paper proof.
9. **Front matter and §1.11, the mechanization boundary** (edition-54
   review, correction 2). The book should not read as saying that every book theorem
   is mechanized: the status table records returned translations, pending
   proofs and two planned statements. For the paper, both parts are now
   checked in Lean, including the inhabitation consequence of the rendering
   theorem (`rendering_inhabitation`); the reference checker is complete on
   the bidirectional fragment only, and the elaboration of omitted grades is
   not implemented.

Items 8 and 9 are proposals for the book's own editing and release process;
this repository does not edit the book.

Nothing else in the book contradicts the paper. In particular, the §5.9
connection passage already agrees with D4: the gathered family keeps the
witness role.

## 4. The two Lean libraries

The textbook's library (`ArtformTextbook`) states the book's mathematics
directly; the paper's (`ArtformRoles`) states the type theory of its roles.
Neither imports the other, and no theorem passes between them. Where both
define a notion of the book, the table says what each one defines and how the
two relate. References to the book are by label.

| Notion | Textbook library | Type-theory library | How they relate |
|---|---|---|---|
| finite form (`def:finite-form`) | `FiniteForm`, with its `FormType` | `FinStr`, a finite structure for a vocabulary of relations (§12.8) | a `FinStr` is a finite form whose carried structure is a family of relations; the calculus interprets erased normal forms as such forms |
| frame and instance (`def:frame-instance`) | `ArtformFrame` and `ArtformFrame.Instance`; `Presentation` holds a frame's positions and values | the frame roles `IsFrame` (F and P); the families `Table` and `Legal` of the capture's signature | the five parts of a frame sit on the four levels of D2: the carrier (FORM), its carried structure (PRESENTATION), the legal states (WITNESS), the moves that act on them (SCRIPT), and the observations, scripts whose values are renderings (D8) |
| legal state (`def:law`, `def:legal-state`) | `Law.Legal` and `ArtformFrame.Instance`: legality is a proposition | `Σ t, Legal t`: legality is proof-relevant | the two counts of states agree exactly when each table has at most one legality proof (`card_states_eq_iff`), as every proposition does: the textbook's count is the book's count through the table (D4) |
| law (`def:law`) | `Law`: invariants and moves on a frame | `Roles.Law`: applications between labels, each with a type of certificates | the paper's law is the counting skeleton of a law: the applications of its moves, and the evidence that admits each |
| history and trajectory (`def:time-history`, `def:trajectory-trace`) | `OccurrenceHistory`, `PerformanceRun.trajectory` | the counts `Law.histories` and `Law.trajectories` | the paper counts the histories and trajectories that the two definitions describe |
| construction (*Writing a construction down*) | no general type; each chapter writes its constructors | `Construction`, a graph with roles and graded dependencies; the terms of the calculus | only the type theory checks the dependency discipline |
| canvas (`def:canvas`, `def:finite-cell-pattern`) | `CellPattern`; `IncidenceCanvas` and the two-dimensional `Canvas` | none | both forms of the canvas are views of a cell pattern (`CellPattern.toIncidenceCanvas`, `Canvas.toIncidenceCanvas`, `Canvas.orientation`); the squares of Chapters 3, 6 and 12 are instances of one geometric form (`SquareForms.one_form`) |
| class function (`def:wilson`) | `IsClassFunction`, defined once and used in Chapters 5 and 11 | none | none |
| `ω = e^{2πi/3}` | `Colour.omega` and `Exact.omega` | none | equal by definition (`Colour.omega_eq_exact`) |
| policy (the word) | `Policy`, the growth policy of `def:policy` | `demand`, the policy matrix `D` | one word with two meanings; the paper calls the matrix the policy `D` |
