# Independent review, 11 October 2026

Reviewed baseline: `d3ba077`, against the pinned 52nd-edition inventory in
`textbook/inventory/edition-52.json`. This is a review of the translations,
their definitions and the guide, with independent checking of the compiled
proofs. It is not a claim that the whole book has been formalized.

**Verdict:** accept the repository for publication as reviewed work in
progress, with the exclusions below. Of 103 statements, 95 translations
receive approval: 91 have full proofs, two have proofs of the author's
explicitly authorized partial scope, and two still have no proof. Six
existing translations are returned; two planned statements cannot yet be
reviewed. Thus the status totals are **91 proved, 2 partial, 8 stated and
2 planned**. A checked proof of a returned translation does not count as a
proof of the book's statement.

## Translations returned for revision

1. **3.32, fair closure** (`prop:fair-closure`,
   `ArtformTextbook/Ch03/Statements/Growth.lean`). The `CadenceFair` definition
   in `Ch03/Defs/Growth.lean` starts pending work when it is enabled. The
   book's Definition 3.30 starts it at its first offer. This changes a
   hypothesis, even though it is a sensible proposed correction. F1 explains
   the ambiguity and counterexample under the policy reading. Keep this
   translation unapproved against edition 52; reconsider after the book's
   fairness definition is settled through its own process.
2. **5.44, Hall's supply theorem** (`thm:hall`,
   `ArtformTextbook/Ch05/Statements.lean`). The book permits
   nonnegative supplies, demands and flows without restricting them to
   whole units. Lean quantifies over natural numbers. A single store and
   shop with supply and demand one half is already outside its scope.
   The later application to whole tokens does not restrict the preceding
   theorem. Extend the theorem to nonnegative real amounts and retain the
   integer result for that application.
3. **6.3, Maxwell covariance** (`thm:maxwell-covariant`,
   `ArtformTextbook/Ch06/Statements/Maxwell.lean`). The macro-tick covariance
   claim is represented. The claim that these data give a law and realizer
   in the sense of Definition 5.35 is not: the statement supplies separate
   moves, invariants and selected batches, without a `Law` or `LawInSteps`
   satisfying the book's per-move legality requirement. This is the actual
   conflict in F2, not a missing routine constructor. Resolve the book's
   definitions before approving this clause.
4. **6.11, the forced shape** (`prop:maxwell-shape`,
   `ArtformTextbook/Ch06/Statements/Maxwell.lean`). Lean starts with a kick
   `faceKick r`, the sum of contributions of one four-edge rule on each
   face. The book starts with an additive link update reading the faces at
   that link. Its proof derives the decomposition into face curvatures
   from gauge blindness before using Gauss's law and uniformity. The
   current encoding chooses a more structured input; it does not establish
   that every allowed link update has that representation. Supply that
   bridge, with a precise interpretation of the book's uniformity clause,
   and keep the existing classification as a lemma. This verdict identifies
   a coverage gap, not a counterexample to the classification.
5. **13.8, third-order transfer** (`thm:ml-transfer`,
   `ArtformTextbook/Ch13/Statements/ThirdOrder.lean`). Its operator theorem
   has a `Fintype G` hypothesis. It therefore cannot supply the SU(3) case
   used by the book. The reduced-resolvent expression is an acceptable
   encoding of the third-order coefficient for the finite groups; a
   theorem for the SU(3) projection and energy operator is still needed.
6. **13.9, transfer values** (`thm:ml-transfer-values`,
   `ArtformTextbook/Ch13/Statements/Transfer.lean`). The four finite models
   use the actual `P V³ P` operator through `transferVal`. The SU(3) clause
   instead uses the explicitly defined matrix `transferWords ... false`.
   Its values and spectra are checked, but their equality to the book's
   projected SU(3) operator is absent; 13.8 cannot supply it while restricted
   to finite groups. Prove that bridge before claiming all five models.

These are exclusions from approval, not newly authorized partial scopes.
The author's partial designations remain limited to 3.43 and 7.4. No Lean
statement or definition was weakened in this review. In particular, the
book conflicts in F1 and F2 are left for the book's separate release process.

## Decisions on the highlighted readings

- **Compatibility and checkpoints:** approve 3.27 and its batch users under
  the declared Section 3.8 profile: disjoint writes, with neither change
  invalidating the other's reads. This is the book's base profile; it is
  not a claim to cover every optional extension of compatibility. The
  boundary interpretation in 5.13 follows the score and its checkpoints.
- **Multiplicity:** in 11.5 and the later finite complex representation
  statements, dimension of equivariant maps is the usual multiplicity.
  This is an equivalent mathematical encoding, not an added assumption.
- **Character formulations:** approve 12.9 and 12.14. The actual triplet
  traces, tensor and symmetric/exterior-square character formulas, degrees
  and pairings determine the stated decompositions. Here the residual
  functions are differences of representation characters; degree and norm
  one identify the positive irreducible character. Norm one for an arbitrary
  class function alone would not suffice. In 13.6 the labelling theorem also
  connects every complete cube family to the five named blocks.
- **SU(3) without Haar integration:** invariant spaces and finite-subgroup
  bounds are appropriate for the counts in 12.2 and 13.2–13.4. There is no
  requirement to introduce Haar measure when these algebraic definitions
  express the claim. This does not establish the missing operator bridge
  in 13.8–13.9.
- **Other model choices:** the real triplets are the displayed real matrix
  representations. Zero matter slots correctly realizes the no-dynamical-
  quark sector of 13.2. The explicit particle-hole map in 12.7 agrees with
  the construction in the book's verifier, including its sign correction.
- **Partial scope:** 3.43 approves the receipt/margin interfaces and the
  stability arithmetic, not their derivation for every feature detector.
  7.4 approves the recorded algebraic core of the native probability law,
  not the full operational derivation. Both remain visibly `partial`.

## Guide and review tooling

**Guide verdict: approve after the corrections in this review.** Part III
now names the independent AI reviewer consistently, reports partial counts
and the returned translations, and distinguishes an approved statement from
a completed proof. It also correctly limits the finiteness claim about
`ZMod n` to positive `n`, and the PDF metadata names the guide itself.
The explanatory source-scanner claim is restricted
to library code, matching what the check actually scans.

Rendered inspection also found the Noether and Stokes quotations overprinted
by their Lean listings, despite a clean LaTeX log. Each of the five short
quotations is now kept in one box, and long listings can break between
lines without a separate page-space reservation. The repaired quotations
and listings were checked on the rebuilt pages.

Two reproducible gaps in the publication checks were fixed:

- An approval previously survived an edit to a book definition or a fidelity
  note. Approval now binds the inventoried book text, definition fidelity
  notes and the statement's declared scope, as well as its Lean surface and
  exact terms. Inventory locations and renumbering alone do not revoke it.
  Prose outside the inventory still requires explicit review when the book
  changes; this checksum does not claim to capture that prose.
- The guide freshness hash previously omitted the generated status appendix's
  inputs. It now includes the manifest, edition, inventory and status renderer.
  An invalid proof report also cannot record an approval or write that appendix.

Regression tests exercise changed definitions and fidelity, legacy approvals,
explicit return verdicts, unchanged/renumbered inputs, Lean dependency changes,
bad proof reports, and changes to the guide's status sources. They run locally
with the publication checks documented in the README.

A clean GitHub run also built all 3,755 jobs and passed the axiom audit,
status, file, regression and PDF freshness checks. The pinned axiom auditor
checked all 8,607 library declarations both locally and on GitHub: only
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` were used. At the author's
request, the CI and release workflows were subsequently removed and GitHub
Actions was disabled. Publication checks now run locally.

Validation: warning-free library/tutorial build; exact proof-target and axiom
report; status and tree/identity checks; independent `leanchecker` replay;
certificate regeneration; guide freshness check; guide rebuild with no LaTeX
errors, overfull boxes or undefined references, followed by rendered-page
inspection. The review changes no Lean definitions, statements or proofs.
