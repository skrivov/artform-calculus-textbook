## Details for the independent judge

For the reading converse, ticks 1, 2 and 3 suffice. Equality at tick 1 gives
`a=a′`; tick 2 gives `ta=t′a`; tick 3 gives
`t²a-t-a=t′²a-t′-a`. Eliminating with `(t+t′)` gives `t=t′`,
without dividing by the reading. Unit rest data prevents the zero-data case.
The closed forms are proved by recurrence uniqueness. The repeated-root
formula works over arbitrary commutative rings when `ε²=1`.

For the Platonic identifications, the finite checks use `decide +kernel`.
They certify all primitive states, uniqueness modulo sign, and every pair's
adjacency, rather than only vertex or edge counts. The icosahedral model has
two poles and two pentagonal rings, with the specified staggered cross edges.
The existing regular-map theorem supplies triangular faces. The independent
judge should check this composition of the graph identifications with the
existing face definition.

For the leapfrog normalization, the source is the actual quotient ring
`S[λ]/(λ²-tλ+1)`. Its map sends `λ` to the parametrized eigenvalue.
The target `F[s,1/(s(s-1)(s-q))]` is an integrally closed domain. Every target
element is integral over the source and is a fraction of source elements,
with the nonzero common denominator `x-1`. These assertions identify it as
the integral closure in the common fraction field, not merely a bijection on
field-valued points. The translated equation has quadratic term `v²-q u²`;
`q≠0` and characteristic other than 2 make its tangent polynomial separable.
The generic proof also verifies the two distinct tangent factors after
adjoining a square root of `q`.

For face/link, write `D=D*` and `(a,b;c,d)` for the face law. Induction on the
score gives `D² | a-1`, `D² | b`, `D² | d-1`, while `c` reduces to the nonzero
constant `κ * totalKick` modulo `D`, so `c` and `D` are coprime. The determinant
and characteristic equations imply `D² | (λ-a)²`. Integral closedness implies
`D | λ-a`, say `λ-a=Dz`. Bezout then gives `(c,z)=(c,Dz)`, hence
`(Dc,λ-a)=(D)(c,λ-a)`. The theorem quantifies over every normal domain
extension, so it applies to the normalization of each irreducible phase-curve
component. It proves the ideal comparison behind forgetting gluing, without
assuming that comparison or postulating a Picard group. The old unconditional
results still cover empty scores and cancelling kicks; only the corrected
source claim about gluedness and the added comparison use nonzero total kick.

Each changed proof file has concrete positive examples in its Non-vacuity
section. The five-state back-step witness is the cycle `(0,1,3,2,4)`; the
unique backward step is `3→2`.
