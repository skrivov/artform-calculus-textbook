## What OutlookOf contributes

The strongest general extension is a precise language for comparing complete processes: evolution relations, embeddings/readouts, allowed preparations, retained detail, boundary/source kernels and compatible quotients. This supports constrained and multivalued situations in which an ordinary reversible update does not describe the entire process. It turns several implicit physical premises into explicit proof obligations.

The checked examples show why those obligations matter. Equal visible spectra or selected readings need not determine complete source response. A forced tail can coexist with a closed visible head; a specified prepared tail can be determined by the current visible pair. Discarding an intermediate variable differs from integrating it with its measure. A classical/projective endpoint omits phase data that a declared coherent reference can detect. Conversely a scalar unitary phase gives the same isolated conjugation channel. The allowed experiment class determines the applicable equivalence claim.

These statements improve the reliability of proposed native transfers. They do not identify the native physical source class, admissible boundary law or gauge quotient by definition. The campaign repeatedly attempted those identifications and records the unresolved premises rather than importing them from finite models.

## The finite quantum and matter results have explicit domains

The convergent Gaussian results are positive real/Euclidean integrals. The finite binary Gauss/Clifford phase pilot is separate from a continuous bosonic metaplectic construction. Its controlled-reference circuit is finite and explicitly specified. The uncertainty-form result proves a Hermitian positivity test and real symplectic dilation pilot, without constructing a completely positive quantum channel or a native boundary state.

The matter addition is a candidate on a constant diagonal coframe chart. Its affine clock leaves the coefficient free and has a nonzero lapse variation at nonzero coupling. The finite heavy regulator is a sixteen-site Euclidean model with an external scalar background and declared Lebesgue measure in its eight normal-coordinate pairs. Exact Walsh/source/difference Grams identify its precision and interaction factors. Actual positive block/product integrals give a temporal/spatial quadratic response gap11/120, error at most8*s^4 per axis, and a strictly positive actual small-amplitude gap. Full axis exchange protects equality in the specified model; spatial symmetry alone does not.

This calculation does not integrate the background over all amplitudes, produce a stable full joint cubic-interaction measure, take a continuum small-momentum limit or measure a limiting speed. The A=B=1,s=2 block is explicitly nonintegrable. Bare-response matching leaves independent parameters. None of these model choices is a native gravitational selection law.

The state-topology example is equally explicit: positive normalized bounded real evaluation functionals on one C([0,1]) space converge on every fixed continuous observable, while their functional-norm distance remains two. It prevents a general upgrade from pointwise/weak-* evidence to a state-norm conclusion. It does not assert that the particular native states fail to converge in norm.
